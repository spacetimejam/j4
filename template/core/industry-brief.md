# Industry brief

A researched picture of how hiring actually works in the field the user is targeting: what recruiters look at first, what a strong application looks like there, and what the market is doing right now. This is what makes the kit's advice field-aware rather than generic.

## How to fill this

Build this immediately after the intake interview, once two inputs exist: the user's CV (in `core/source/`) and their answer to what they want next (intake step 2). Research the target field with current web sources: hiring guides, recruiter commentary, job adverts for the target roles, sector press. Prefer practitioner and recruiter sources over generic careers content. Summarise findings here in your own words, with the date, and note the main sources.

Research the field the user is *aiming at*, not only the one they are in. If the two differ, cover both and note the differences.

Keep it honest about uncertainty. If sources disagree or evidence is thin, say so rather than presenting a guess as a norm.

When the draft is ready, present it to the user for review, like any other core document. The brief steers nothing until they have read it, corrected it where their own knowledge of the field is better, and confirmed adopting it. **[STOP]**

## What this brief changes, and what it never changes

The brief **informs**:

- **CV emphasis and structure.** Which sections lead, how much space evidence types get (portfolio, certifications, service metrics, client names), and what recruiters in this field scan for first.
- **Cover letter conventions.** Register, length norms, and what the field expects a letter to prove.
- **Fit judgments.** Market salary ranges, what counts as a seniority signal in this field, and how transferable the user's experience reads to someone hiring in it.
- **Interview prep.** Field-typical formats (tastings, trade tests, portfolio walkthroughs, service scenarios), the questions this field asks, and current sector talking points.

The brief **never overrides**:

- Accuracy. No convention justifies inventing or inflating evidence.
- The user's voice (`core/voice.md`) and their fit bar and dealbreakers (`core/profile.md`). The brief describes the market; the user decides what they want from it.
- The workflow's confirmation checkpoints and the kit's hard rendering rules.

When an application sits outside the researched field (adjacent sector, career pivot), do not stretch this brief to cover it. Do a quick delta research for that field as part of the application's `fit.md`, and fold anything durable back in here.

## Keeping it current

Re-check the brief when the market read starts contradicting what applications are experiencing, or when the search shifts towards a different field. On top of those triggers, when a new application starts and the researched date is more than twelve months old, renew the research before the fit read. Update in place and refresh the researched date; move superseded readings to a short history note at the bottom if they explain past decisions.
